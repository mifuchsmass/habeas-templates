================================================================================
           WORD TEMPLATE AUTOMATION GUIDE FOR HABEAS PLEADINGS
================================================================================

This guide explains how to format Microsoft Word (.docx) templates so our
habeas petition generator can automatically insert petitioner details, 
court headers, pronoun variations, and conditional legal paragraphs.


1. HOW PLACEHOLDERS WORK (FILLING IN BLANKS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Whenever you want the generator to fill in information from the form, wrap the
field name in square brackets: [Field Name]

Available Name Fields:
• [Petitioner Name]           -> Combines First, Middle, Last, and Suffix
                                 (e.g., "Jane M. Doe, Jr.")
• [Petitioner First Name]     -> "Jane"
• [Petitioner Middle Name]    -> "M."
• [Petitioner Last Name]      -> "Doe"
• [Petitioner Suffix]         -> "Jr."

Other Common Fields:
• [Defendant Name] or [Respondent Name]
• [Case Number]
• [Jurisdiction]

Key Features for Legal Drafting:
• Smart Caps:
  - If you type the placeholder in ALL CAPS: [PETITIONER NAME]
    -> The generated document outputs: JANE M. DOE
  - If you type the placeholder normally: [Petitioner Name]
    -> The generated document outputs: Jane M. Doe
• Missing Data Warning: If someone forgets to enter a field on the web form, the 
  document will print "*** MISSING DATA ***" in that spot so incomplete pleadings 
  are never accidentally filed with the court.
• Curly Quotes: Word automatically turns straight quotes into curly "smart quotes." 
  The engine handles this automatically without breaking.


2. AUTOMATIC PRONOUN & GENDER TOGGLING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The intake form includes a 1-click pronoun selector (He/Him, She/Her, They/Them, 
or Petitioner/Noun Only). When a selection is made, the generator automatically 
derives and populates all grammatical pronoun forms.

A. Recommended Tag Formats
You can use either intuitive slash tags or direct pronoun tags:

• Subject (lowercase):       [he], [he/she], or [pronoun subject]
• Subject (Capitalized):     [He], [He/She], or [Pronoun Subject]
• Subject (ALL CAPS):        [HE] or [HE/SHE]
• Object (lowercase):        [him], [him/her], or [pronoun object]
• Object (Capitalized):      [Him], [Him/Her], or [Pronoun Object]
• Object (ALL CAPS):         [HIM] or [HIM/HER]
• Possessive (lowercase):    [his], [his/her], or [pronoun possessive]
• Possessive (Capitalized):  [His], [His/Her], or [Pronoun Possessive]
• Possessive (ALL CAPS):     [HIS] or [HIS/HER]
• Standalone Possessive:     [hers], [his/hers]
• Reflexive:                 [himself], [himself/herself], or [pronoun reflexive]

B. Output Reference Matrix

| Tag Typed in Word | He / Him (Default) | She / Her | They / Them | Petitioner (Noun) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| [he/she] or [he]   | he                 | she       | they        | Petitioner        |
| [He/She] or [He]   | He                 | She       | They        | Petitioner        |
| [HE/SHE] or [HE]   | HE                 | SHE       | THEY        | PETITIONER        |
| [his/her] or [his] | his                | her       | their       | Petitioner's      |
| [His/Her] or [His] | His                | Her       | Their       | Petitioner's      |
| [HIS/HER] or [HIS] | HIS                | HER       | THEIR       | PETITIONER'S      |
| [him/her] or [him] | him                | her       | them        | Petitioner        |
| [himself/herself]  | himself            | herself   | themselves  | Petitioner        |

C. Verb Agreement Helpers
When drafting sentences with verbs that change between singular (he/she) and 
plural (they), use these agreement tags:
• [is/are]   -> Resolves to "is" (male/female/petitioner) or "are" (nonbinary)
• [was/were] -> Resolves to "was" (male/female/petitioner) or "were" (nonbinary)
• [has/have] -> Resolves to "has" (male/female/petitioner) or "have" (nonbinary)

Example Pleading Sentence:
  [He/She] [is/are] currently detained in administrative custody. [He/She] 
  [has/have] remained confined without an individualized bond hearing, violating 
  [his/her] due process rights.

D. Conditional Pronoun Checks
You can also write conditional blocks based on the selected pronoun option:
  [IF Pronouns = "male"]
  Petitioner uses he/him pronouns.
  [ELSE IF Pronouns = "female"]
  Petitioner uses she/her pronouns.
  [ELSE IF Pronouns = "nonbinary"]
  Petitioner uses gender-neutral they/them pronouns.
  [ELSE]
  Petitioner is referred to exclusively by title as "Petitioner."
  [END IF]


3. CONDITIONAL RULES (CHOOSING WHICH LANGUAGE TO INCLUDE)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
You can tell Word to show or hide language based on the case type, state, or 
court selected.

A. Showing Language Only When Applicable ([IF] ... [END IF])
Use this when language should only appear under one specific condition.

Example:
  [IF Jurisdiction = "Massachusetts"]
  See Mass. Gen. Laws ch. 260, § 2.
  [END IF]

B. Choosing Between Two Options ([IF] ... [ELSE] ... [END IF])
Use this when you want Option A for one scenario, and Option B for everything else.

Example:
  [IF Type of Habeas Petition = "GO Class"]
  Petitioner brings this action on behalf of all similarly situated individuals.
  [ELSE]
  Petitioner brings this action individually.
  [END IF]

C. Choosing Among Multiple Options ([IF] ... [ELSE IF] ... [ELSE] ... [END IF])
Use this when you have three or more distinct choices.

Example (Court Header):
  [IF Jurisdiction = "Massachusetts"]
  IN THE UNITED STATES DISTRICT COURT FOR THE DISTRICT OF MASSACHUSETTS
  [ELSE IF Jurisdiction = "Rhode Island"]
  IN THE UNITED STATES DISTRICT COURT FOR THE DISTRICT OF RHODE ISLAND
  [ELSE]
  IN THE UNITED STATES DISTRICT COURT FOR THE DISTRICT OF [JURISDICTION]
  [END IF]


4. COMBINING CONDITIONS & COMPARISONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
You can write natural conditions using everyday legal logic:

• Equals:                   [IF Jurisdiction = "Massachusetts"]
• Does NOT equal:           [IF Jurisdiction != "Massachusetts"]
• Both must be true (AND):  [IF Jurisdiction = "Massachusetts" and Petition Type = "GO Class"]
• Either can be true (OR):  [IF Jurisdiction = "Massachusetts" or Jurisdiction = "Rhode Island"]
• Numeric Comparisons:      [IF Days In Detention >= 180]


5. HOW TO AVOID EXTRA BLANK LINES AND KEEP AUTO-NUMBERING INTACT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
To make sure Word's automatic paragraph numbering (1, 2, 3, 4...) stays in 
consecutive order without leaving orphan numbers or blank lines, follow these 
two simple rules:

RULE 1: Changing Words or Citations INSIDE a Paragraph
If the numbered paragraph will ALWAYS exist, but the text inside it changes, 
keep the tags on the exact same line:

  3. This action is brought pursuant to [Jurisdiction] law. [IF Jurisdiction = "Massachusetts"]See Mass. Gen. Laws ch. 260, § 2.[ELSE]See applicable statutory provisions.[END IF]

RULE 2: Making an ENTIRE Numbered Paragraph Optional
If a paragraph should disappear completely when not needed (and not leave behind 
an empty "4." or blank space):
• Place [IF ...] on its own unnumbered line above the paragraph.
• Put your legal text on the numbered line.
• Place [END IF] on its own unnumbered line below the paragraph.

Example:
  [IF Type of Habeas Petition = "GO Class"]
  4. Petitioner brings this action on behalf of all similarly situated parties.
  [END IF]

  What happens:
  - If "GO Class" is selected: Paragraph 4 is rendered normally.
  - If "Non-GO" is selected: Paragraph 4 is completely removed, and the next 
    paragraph automatically becomes paragraph 4.


6. NESTING RULES (RULES INSIDE RULES)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
You can place one rule inside another rule. Just ensure that every [IF] has a 
corresponding [END IF] at the end:

  [IF Jurisdiction = "Massachusetts"]
    [IF Type of Habeas Petition = "GO Class"]
      Massachusetts Class Action Claims
    [ELSE]
      Massachusetts Individual Claims
    [END IF]
  [ELSE]
    Out-of-District Claims
  [END IF]
